\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Data.} \dataset{Swissmetro} ($9{,}036$ tasks, $1{,}004$ respondents),
\dataset{Optima} ($1{,}906$ trips, $1{,}486$ respondents), \dataset{LPMC} ($3{,}556$ London
trips, $454$ households) and \dataset{Amazon} ($1{,}183$ purchases, $99$ respondents), the
last because its alternatives are catalogue products. Travel text comes from a frozen $70$B
model and \dataset{Amazon} text from a local $8$B model, so its absolute numbers are not
comparable across domains while its internal contrasts are. Three seeds per travel dataset.

\Cref{tab:benchmarks} places the specification among nine references: lowest log-loss on
\dataset{Optima} and \dataset{LPMC}, by $0.028$ and $0.034$ nats over the best reference, and
beaten on \dataset{Swissmetro} by gradient boosting ($0.574$ against $0.609$). Two features
matter more than the ranking. The attribute channel alone attains most of it, so the language
channel accounts for $0.008$, $-0.001$ and $0.028$ nats of the three totals. And under the
chronological within-person split this literature more commonly uses, the same comparison puts
the proposed specification ahead on all three datasets; the ranking is a property of the
protocol as much as of the models (\Cref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{Combination, restatement, content}

A second channel with no text is worth $0.011$ on \dataset{LPMC} and $0.019$ on
\dataset{Optima}: combination alone accounts for a third to all of what is usually attributed
to language. Against that line the generated text adds $0.021$ on \dataset{LPMC} and nothing
on \dataset{Optima}. Templated facts recover most of the gain everywhere, which bounds what
the generated content can be worth.

The fitted weight is not a measure of content. The text channel receives $\pi = 0.21$--$0.40$;
a parameter-matched channel containing no text receives $0.37$--$0.52$; under the
time-respecting split the text-free channel takes $0.85$ against the text's $0.70$. Weights
for every channel are in \Cref{tab:pi}.

\subsection{The weight-matched uninformative control}

Replacing the text channel by the uniform distribution at the same fitted $\pi$
(\Cref{tab:floor}, \Cref{app:extra}) leaves \dataset{LPMC} and \dataset{Optima} worse by $0.13$
and $0.18$ nats, so there the weight is buying content. On \dataset{Swissmetro} the difference
is $-0.001$ and on \dataset{Amazon}, where the channel predicts at chance ($1.593$ against
$1.609$), it is $0.018$ of a total gain of $0.114$: in those two the pooled gain is the bound
of \Cref{eq:floor}.

\Cref{tab:identification} is the central result. An identified contribution survives on three
of the four datasets and not on the fourth, and the amount erasure removes differs sharply
between them.

On \dataset{LPMC} the unerased channel beats its control by $0.030$ and $0.017$ survives
erasure, so $43\%$ of the raw gain was linearly recoverable from the attributes and the rest
was not. On \dataset{Swissmetro} the contribution is smaller, $0.009$, but erasure costs
\emph{nothing}: the unerased and erased channels are within $0.0003$ of each other, so what the
text contributes there was never a restatement to begin with. On \dataset{Amazon} erasure
\emph{improves} the channel, from $0.020$ to $0.042$, which is what one expects when the
generated text encodes an attribute the model already has -- the probe of
\Cref{sec:identification} recovers half the held-out price variance from that text -- and
removing that direction leaves the reader only what is not duplicated. On \dataset{Optima}
every contrast has an interval containing zero, the largest being $+0.0016$: there is no
event-specific content to identify, and the small pooled gain in \Cref{tab:main} is
combination.

The ranking by identified content differs from the ranking by unerased difference, so the
pooled numbers do not indicate which datasets have content. The \dataset{Amazon} row rests on
one seed, that dataset having one exported seed, with the interval from clustering over $99$
respondents rather than over seeds.

\subsection{Architecture}

The axis-preserving reader improves on the pooled reader by $0.012$ on \dataset{LPMC} and
$0.006$ on \dataset{Swissmetro}, both with intervals excluding zero, and its factors can be
inspected. Neither designed reader beats an ordinary two-layer network given the same axis
slots. We therefore do not claim the architecture as a contribution. Removing one component at a time
(\Cref{tab:knockouts}, \Cref{app:extra}) shows that only the low-rank projection survives inside
the pool; the personalised weights, the attention, the auxiliary loss and the slot dropout each
change the pooled score by less than $0.004$.

\subsection{Protocol dependence}

Under the respondent-disjoint split the generated text is the best channel; under the
time-respecting split on the same data a shuffled control beats it and a text-free channel
beats both. Both are defensible questions, and they give opposite answers. A paper reporting
one of them reports half a result.
