\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Data.} Three travel-mode datasets -- \dataset{Swissmetro} ($9{,}036$ stated-preference
tasks, $1{,}004$ respondents), \dataset{Optima} ($1{,}906$ reported trips, $1{,}486$ respondents),
\dataset{LPMC} ($3{,}556$ London trips, $600$ respondents in $454$ households) -- and one retail
dataset, \dataset{Amazon} ($1{,}183$ purchases, $99$ respondents), which we include because its
alternatives are catalogue products rather than a fixed set and so stress the pipeline's
assumptions. Sentences for the travel datasets come from a frozen $70$B model; the
\dataset{Amazon} sentences come from a locally hosted $8$B model, so its absolute numbers are not
comparable with the others while its internal contrasts are. Three master seeds per travel
dataset, one for \dataset{Amazon}. Test sets are never touched until configurations are frozen.

A second numeric channel with no text is worth $0.011$ on \dataset{LPMC} and $0.019$ on
\dataset{Optima}: ensembling alone accounts for a third to all of what is usually attributed to
language. Against that line the generated text adds $0.021$ on \dataset{LPMC} and nothing on
\dataset{Optima}. Templated facts recover most of the gain everywhere, which bounds what the
\emph{generated} content can be worth.

The fitted mixture weight is not a measure of content. The language channel receives
$\pi = 0.21$--$0.40$ across datasets, while a parameter-matched channel containing no text at all
receives $0.37$--$0.52$, and under the time-respecting split of \Cref{tab:protocol} the text-free
channel takes $0.85$ against the text's $0.70$. Weights for every channel are in \Cref{tab:pi}.

On \dataset{LPMC} and \dataset{Optima} the language channel is worth $0.13$ and $0.18$ nats more
than a uniform distribution at the same weight: the content is real. On \dataset{Swissmetro} it
is worth $-0.001$, and on \dataset{Amazon}, where the channel predicts at chance ($1.593$ against
$1.609$), it is worth $0.018$ of a total mixture gain of $0.114$. In those two cases the gain is
the bound in \Cref{eq:floor}, not information, and it is invisible to the controls in common use
because those controls are themselves trained models.

by $0.012$ on \dataset{LPMC} and $0.006$ on \dataset{Swissmetro}, both with intervals excluding
zero, and it is the version whose factors can be inspected. Neither designed reader beats a

interpretability rather than accuracy, and \Cref{tab:knockouts} locates what inside it matters.

Under the respondent-disjoint split the generated text is the best channel; under the
time-respecting split on the same data a shuffled control beats it and a text-free channel beats
both. Both protocols are defensible questions -- predicting for a new person, and predicting a
known person's next trip -- and they give opposite answers. A paper reporting only one of them
reports half a result.
