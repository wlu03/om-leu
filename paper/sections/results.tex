\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Data.} \dataset{Swissmetro} ($9{,}036$ tasks, $1{,}004$ respondents),
\dataset{Optima} ($1{,}906$ trips, $1{,}486$ respondents), \dataset{LPMC} ($3{,}556$ London
trips, $454$ households) and \dataset{Amazon} ($1{,}183$ purchases, $99$ respondents), the
last because its alternatives are catalogue products. Travel text comes from a frozen $70$B
model and \dataset{Amazon} text from a local $8$B model, so its absolute numbers are not
comparable across domains while its internal contrasts are. Three seeds per travel dataset.

A second channel with no text is worth $0.011$ on \dataset{LPMC} and $0.019$ on
\dataset{Optima}: combination alone accounts for a third to all of what is usually attributed
to language. Against that line the generated text adds $0.021$ on \dataset{LPMC} and nothing
on \dataset{Optima}. Templated facts recover most of the gain everywhere, which bounds what
the generated content can be worth.

The fitted weight is not a measure of content. The text channel receives $\pi = 0.21$--$0.40$;
a parameter-matched channel containing no text receives $0.37$--$0.52$; under the
time-respecting split the text-free channel takes $0.85$ against the text's $0.70$. Weights
for every channel are in \Cref{tab:pi}.

\Cref{tab:identification} is the central result. On \dataset{LPMC} the unerased channel beats
its control by $0.030$; after erasure $0.017$ remains, with the interval excluding zero. The
difference between those two numbers is the part of the raw gain that was linearly recoverable
from the attributes: $40\%$ of it. Training against the attribute model's own predictions
gives a larger surviving difference still. On \dataset{Optima} every contrast has an interval
containing zero, the largest being $+0.0016$: there is no event-specific content to identify,
and the small gain visible in \Cref{tab:main} is combination.

\subsection{Architecture}

The axis-preserving reader improves on the pooled reader by $0.012$ on \dataset{LPMC} and
$0.006$ on \dataset{Swissmetro}, both with intervals excluding zero, and its factors can be
inspected. Neither designed reader beats an ordinary two-layer network given the same axis
slots. We therefore do not claim the architecture as a contribution. Removing one component at a time
(\Cref{tab:knockouts}, \Cref{app:extra}) shows that only the low-rank projection survives inside
the pool; the personalised weights, the attention, the auxiliary loss and the slot dropout each
change the pooled score by less than $0.004$.
