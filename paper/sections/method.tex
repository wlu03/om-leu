\section{The system under test}
\label{sec:method}

We hold a three-stage pipeline fixed and vary one component at a time. The pipeline predicts
which alternative $j$ a person $i$ chooses from an available set, given tabulated attributes
$x_{ij}$, person covariates $z_i$, admissible history $h_{ij}$, and $K$ generated sentences per
alternative.

\paragraph{Stage 1: structural utility.} A random-utility model with covariate-dependent
coefficients,
\begin{equation}
V_{ij} = \mathrm{ASC}_j + \beta^{\top} x_{ij} - \beta_t(z_i)\, t_{ij} - \beta_c(z_i)\, c_{ij}
         + g_j(z_i) + u_{p(i)j},
\end{equation}
where $\beta_t,\beta_c > 0$ are produced by a small network through a softplus, $g_j$ is a
zero-initialised network of the covariates, and $u_{pj}$ is an $L_2$-penalised person effect.
The sign restriction on time and cost is a modelling assumption about tastes, not a requirement
of random utility. Five fits from different initialisations are averaged at the probability
level, giving $L_{ij} = \log \frac{1}{R}\sum_r \mathrm{softmax}_j(V^{(r)}_i)$.

\paragraph{Stage 2: shape-constrained residual.} One gradient-boosted ensemble per alternative
reads that alternative's own attributes, the covariates and the history, and is trained under
the choice-set softmax with $\tau L_{ij}$ as an offset:
$U_{ij} = \tau L_{ij} + f_j(x_{ij}, z_i, h_{ij})$. Time and cost carry monotone constraints
inside $f_j$. The offset is cross-fitted on inner folds so that the residual does not correct a
model that has already seen the same rows. We note that a constraint inside one component does
not make the final mixture monotone in an attribute; \Cref{sec:gate} gives the missing term.

\paragraph{Stage 3: the language channel.} A frozen language model writes $K=5$ first-person
sentences per (person, alternative), one per axis -- financial, time, comfort, convenience,
reliability -- from the attributes, covariates and admissible history. A frozen sentence encoder
maps each to $\mathbb{R}^{768}$. One member of the reader projects to $r=32$ dimensions with a
layer norm, pools the $K$ sentences by content-based attention, reads $M=5$ linear heads from the
pooled vector, and combines them with weights $w(z_i)$ on the simplex:
$\tilde V_{ij} = \sum_m w_m(z_i) A_{ijm}$. Five members with different seeds are averaged in
probability to give $\bar p_i(j)$.

\paragraph{Combination.} The channels meet at the probability level,
\begin{equation}
p_i(j) = (1-\pi)\,\mathrm{softmax}_j(a\,U_i) + \pi\, \bar p_i(j),
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
with $\pi = \sigma(\gamma)$ and inverse temperature $a$. These two scalars are the \emph{only}
parameters that couple the channels, which is what makes the language contribution exactly
measurable: setting $\pi = 0$ recovers the conventional model. Everything else is frozen before
they are estimated.

\label{sec:axis}
\paragraph{An axis-preserving reader.} We also evaluate a variant that scores each axis before
any pooling: $c_{ijk} = g_k(\mathrm{LN}(W e_{ijk}))$ with head $k$ reading only sentence slot
$k$, then $u_{ij} = s_i \sum_k w_k(z_i)\, c_{ijk}$. We verify by construction that a
perturbation of one axis score changes the utility only through its own weighted term, and that
the model is equivariant to permuting alternatives (\Cref{app:tests}). This variant is
interpretable in a way the pooled reader is not; \Cref{sec:results} reports what it costs or
buys.
