\section{Outcome-conditioned utility}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/architecture}
\caption{The pipeline held fixed throughout. Stages 1 and 2 form the attribute channel;
stage 3 is the text channel. The two meet only through the scalars $\pi$ and $a$, so setting
$\pi = 0$ recovers the attribute model exactly and the text contribution is measurable without

\label{fig:architecture}

\paragraph{Formulation.} For event $i$ with available set $\mathcal{A}_i$, attributes
$x_{ij}$, covariates $z_i$ and admissible history $h_{ij}$, write the systematic utility as an
expectation over an unobserved consequence distribution $\mathcal{O}^\star_{ij}$,
\begin{equation}
V_{ij} = \mathbb{E}_{o \sim \mathcal{O}^\star_{ij}}\!\left[u(o, z_i)\right],
\qquad
\mathcal{O}^\star_{ij} = \mathcal{O}(\,\cdot \mid x_{ij}, z_i, h_{ij}),
\label{eq:ideal}
\end{equation}
with choice probabilities from the conditional logit. Two things are unavailable:
$\mathcal{O}^\star$ is never observed, and the expectation is intractable. The modelling
question is what to substitute, and the identification question is how one would know the
substitute carries anything.

\paragraph{Measurement equation.} A frozen language model is used as a proposal distribution
over consequences: for each $(i,j)$ it emits $K$ short first-person sentences, one per
prompt-defined axis, from $(x_{ij}, z_i, h_{ij})$ rendered as text. A frozen encoder maps each
to $e_{ijk} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. This is the measurement equation of the formulation, and it
is the part a choice modeller would ask about: it has no free parameters, its normalisation is
the encoder's, and it is not estimated jointly with the utility.

\paragraph{Reader.} A member projects each embedding to $r$ dimensions with a layer norm,
scores it, and combines the scores with weights on the simplex:
$\tilde V_{ij} = s_i \sum_k w_k(z_i)\, c_{ijk}$, where $c_{ijk}$ is a scalar read from slot $k$
alone. Five members with different seeds are averaged in probability to give $\bar p_i(j)$. We
verify by construction that perturbing one axis score changes the utility only through its own
weighted term, and that the model is equivariant to permuting alternatives
(\Cref{app:tests}). We report in \Cref{sec:results} what this structure is worth against an
ordinary network, and the answer is: nothing.

\paragraph{Attribute channel.} Stage 1 is a random-utility model with covariate-dependent
time and cost coefficients, functional intercepts and penalised person effects, averaged over
five fits at the probability level. Stage 2 adds one shape-constrained boosted ensemble per
alternative on that alternative's own block, with the stage-1 log-probability as a cross-fitted
offset. Neither is our contribution; both are held fixed.

\paragraph{Combination.} The channels meet at the probability level,
\begin{equation}
p_i(j) = (1-\pi)\,\mathrm{softmax}_j(a\,U_i) + \pi\, \bar p_i(j),
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
with $\pi = \sigma(\gamma)$ and inverse temperature $a$. These two scalars are the only
parameters coupling the channels, which is what makes the text contribution exactly
measurable. We note explicitly that the attribute channel is \emph{not} routed through the
text representation: \Cref{eq:mixture} is a pool of two channels, not a bottleneck, and any
claim that all information reaches the utility through the embeddings would be false of this
model. That combination is the side-channel of \citet{havasi2022addressing} in the pooled
form, and \Cref{sec:results} reports the text channel scored with $\pi$ fixed at one as well
as at its fitted value.

\paragraph{What is estimated by hand, and why we removed it.} A natural way to strengthen the
attribute channel is to multiply its gradient by a constant chosen on a sweep. We do not do
this. A hyperparameter swept against visible performance makes the split between channels a
researcher degree of freedom, and the resulting headline can be a monotone function of that
constant while the text channel's own accuracy stays flat. We fit $\pi$ and $a$ on out-of-fold
predictions instead (\Cref{sec:protocol}), which is standard \citep{vanderlaan2007super} and
which we treat as hygiene rather than as a contribution.
