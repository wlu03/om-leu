\section{Four proposals that did not work}
\label{sec:negative}

Each of the following was motivated by a diagnosis of why the text channel underperforms; we
report them because the diagnoses are testable and the failures are informative.
\Cref{tab:improvements} in \Cref{app:extra} gives the four numbers.

\paragraph{Grounded generation.} A prompt that tags every sentence as observed, derived or
unknown and forbids invented quantities, affordability judgements and demographic inferences,
with programmatic validators for schema, evidence vocabulary and numeral support. Running it at
scale needs a generation budget we did not have, so the comparison is recorded as blocked rather
than as a negative result. What we can report is that templated facts predict better than the
generated text whenever the channel is scored alone.

\paragraph{Consistency training.} If the representation tracks outcome magnitudes rather than
wording, raising a fare should lower the financial score and leave the time score alone, and a
claim-preserving paraphrase should move nothing. We built an edit registry with units,
eligibility rules and declared affected and unaffected axes, and paraphrase families preserving
quantities, negation polarity and evidence status, holding out families and magnitudes for
evaluation. Training on these objectives produced no predictive gain. More informative than the
score: on held-out edit types the score moves in the wrong direction on $25$--$43\%$ of eligible
events, and the ordering loss barely changes that. The representation is not behaviourally
consistent, and making it more consistent did not make it more accurate.

\paragraph{Complementarity-aware training.} Fine-tuning on
$-\log((1-\pi_{\text{train}}) p^{\text{attr}}_{y} + \pi_{\text{train}} q_{y})$ with the
attribute side detached and produced by inner folds inside the outer training partition. The
gradient identity $\nabla \mathcal{L} = \rho\,\nabla(-\log q_y)$ with
$\rho = \pi q_y / ((1-\pi)p_y + \pi q_y)$ is verified against autograd. The pool did not
improve and the standalone scores became worse. Note that the erasure device of
\Cref{sec:identification} targets a different quantity -- restatement rather than redundancy --
and does produce a measurable effect.

\paragraph{Conditional weight.} A regularised logistic gate over history length, a missingness
indicator and choice-set size, fitted only on held-out channel predictions and shrunk towards
the global weight, spread the per-event weight from $0.15$ to $0.58$ and predicted no better
than a single number. The control that matters: the same gate over a channel with no text
scored $0.009$ worse, so gate capacity is not a hidden source of gain. For any claim about
monotonicity of the complete pipeline, note that
$\partial p_j/\partial x = (1-\pi)\,\partial q_j/\partial x + \pi\,\partial r_j/\partial x +
(r_j - q_j)\,\partial \pi/\partial x$; a sign constraint inside the residual binds only the
first term.

\subsection{Why a component can matter alone and not in the pool}
\label{sec:why-weights}

The largest knock-out disappears when the channel is pooled, and the reason is measurable
rather than rhetorical. The personalisation is genuine: a variant that learns \emph{one} weight
vector shared by everybody recovers none of the loss. But the loss is a tail rather than a
shift, those tail events are ones the attribute model already ranks correctly, and
\Cref{eq:floor} bounds what such a failure can cost in the pool. Measured attenuation is
$25.9\times$ on \dataset{Optima} and $21.9\times$ on \dataset{LPMC}. The component raises
average accuracy without changing which events the text channel wins, and that is what a
probability pool discards. \Cref{app:weights} gives the numbers.

\subsection{Transfer and sample size}

A scorer trained on one dataset and frozen is worse than fitting on the target in all six
ordered directions; fine-tuning recovers most of the difference, so the transferred parameters
are a reasonable initialisation rather than a transferable notion of outcome level. Learning
curves subsampling whole households show templated facts leading generated text at every
training size. Both tables are in \Cref{app:extra}.
