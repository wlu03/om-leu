\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} On three of four datasets the generated text carries a
contribution that survives erasure of the attribute span and its own shuffled control:
$0.017$ nats on \dataset{LPMC}, $0.009$ on \dataset{Swissmetro}, $0.042$ on \dataset{Amazon}
at one seed. On \dataset{Optima} it does not. So generated consequence text can carry
choice-relevant information beyond the attributes it was generated from, and often does.

Two qualifications. The amounts are small next to what combination alone supplies, and on two
datasets the \emph{pooled} gain is still mostly the bound of \Cref{eq:floor} rather than the
identified content: a channel can carry real information and still owe most of its apparent
benefit to mixing. And the ranking by identified content differs from the ranking by pooled
gain, so the pooled number does not indicate which datasets have content.

\paragraph{What it does not support.} No causal identification: nothing tests the sequential
ignorability that \emph{mediation} would require \citep{imai2010general}, which is why we avoid
the word. No psychological identification: the axes are prompt labels and the weights fitted
scalars, and tying them to what people attend to needs elicitation from people. No
architectural claim, no equivalence from intervals containing zero, and the identification is
linear in scope.

\paragraph{Reporting.} Report the fitted weight beside the weight a text-free channel
attracts; include a weight-matched uninformative control, since the usual controls are trained
models and miss \Cref{eq:floor}; and state which split a number comes from, since the two
defensible splits disagree here in direction, not magnitude.

Limitations -- small public datasets, two incomplete arms, linear identification -- and the two
designs that would convert this into a claim about perception are in \Cref{app:limits}.
