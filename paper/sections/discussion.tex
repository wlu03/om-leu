\section{Discussion and conclusion}

\paragraph{What we think the evidence supports.} Generated consequence text carries information
beyond the recorded attributes on two of four datasets, and that information survives every
control we could construct, including the uniform floor. It is not large: $0.02$--$0.03$ nats in
the deployed system, against $0.01$--$0.02$ available to any second predictor. It is not
architectural: an ordinary network reads the same embeddings as well as our designed readers do.
And it is not robust to the evaluation question: on the one dataset where both protocols are
legitimate, changing from ``a new person'' to ``this person's next trip'' reverses the ranking of
the real text and a shuffled control.

\paragraph{What it does not support.} No causal identification: the edited events in
\Cref{sec:negative} carry no observed choice. No psychological identification: the attribute heads
are named, not identified, and the mixture weight is a fitted scalar. No equivalence from intervals
containing zero, and no monotonicity for the complete pipeline.

\paragraph{Recommendations for reporting.} Three would have changed our own earlier conclusions:
report the mixture weight next to the weight a text-free channel attracts, since the latter is
usually larger; include a uniform-floor control, since the usual controls are themselves trained
models and miss the bound in \Cref{eq:floor}; and state which split a number comes from, since on
our data the two defensible splits disagree in direction, not magnitude.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The travel datasets are public case studies with a few hundred to a
thousand respondents, and the intervals condition on the observed split. The \dataset{Amazon} run
uses a smaller generator on a hundred-respondent fixture, so it establishes the floor effect in a
second domain but nothing quantitative across domains. Two arms are incomplete: the
grounded-generation comparison is blocked on generation budget, and an extension representing
outcomes as distributions is implemented and tested on synthetic fixtures but has no empirical
arm, since no dataset records repeated journeys, scenario probabilities or elicited beliefs.

Separating ensembling, content and architecture, and adding a control that isolates the
arithmetic of mixing, leaves a smaller and more conditional claim than the usual one: real content
on some datasets, none on others, no architectural advantage, and a direction that depends on
which prediction question is asked. The controls and the protocol are cheap to adopt.
