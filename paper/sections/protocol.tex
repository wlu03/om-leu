\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Splits}

\paragraph{Respondent-disjoint (primary).} Every event of a respondent, and of a household
where the identifier encodes one, lies in one partition, and no respondent-specific effect is
fitted for a held-out respondent. This is the question of predicting for a person not seen
before. It matters here because the reader takes person covariates in two places, so a split
that shares respondents between training and test gives the text channel two routes to
memorise identity.

\paragraph{Time-respecting (secondary).} We first check that the ordering field is calendar
time. Of three travel datasets only one passes: \dataset{LPMC} carries $3{,}371$ distinct
timestamps over three years, \dataset{Swissmetro}'s ordering field is the presentation index
of a questionnaire, and \dataset{Optima}'s dates were manufactured by a preparation script and
take four values. We report this protocol on \dataset{LPMC} only and record the others as
unsupported with that reason.

\subsection{Nested calibration}

The scalars in \Cref{eq:mixture} are fitted by L-BFGS on predictions neither channel trained
on. Five outer folds cover the development partition; for each fold the entire pipeline is
rebuilt on the complement and predicts the held-out fold; the scalars are fitted on the pooled
out-of-fold predictions; the stages are refitted on the whole development partition and the
locked test partition is scored once. Folds hold out whole respondents when the test regime is
respondent-disjoint and events when it shares respondents. The temperature is applied exactly
once, enforced by a test, and the attribute-only baseline receives its own temperature fitted
on the same held-out data with the same budget, so it is not handicapped by being
uncalibrated.

\subsection{A ladder of controls}
\label{sec:controls}

Each control removes one explanation, in increasing strength: a parameter-matched second
channel with \emph{no} text (isolates combination); shuffled sentences from a different event
matched on dataset, alternative and axis, with donors drawn only from the fitting partition
(isolates event-specific from generic content); identity-only text; seeded random unit
vectors; a deterministic template built from exactly the fields the generator may see
(isolates what the language adds beyond restating the recorded quantities); and the
weight-matched uninformative channel.

The last is the one we did not find a precedent for. Because
$p_i(y) \geq (1-\pi)\, q_i(y)$,
\begin{equation}
-\log p_i(y) \;\leq\; -\log q_i(y) \;-\; \log(1-\pi),
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
so mixing in \emph{any} distribution bounds the loss on events where the attribute model is
confidently wrong: $0.36$ nats per event at $\pi = 0.3$, available to a channel that predicts
at chance. The first five controls are trained models producing non-uniform distributions and
so do not isolate this; replacing the text channel by the uniform distribution \emph{at the
same fitted $\pi$} does.

\subsection{Metrics}

The primary metric is mean per-event negative log-likelihood; Brier score, top-1 accuracy and
a fifteen-bin top-label calibration error are recorded. Differences are paired per event,
reported as baseline minus variant so that positive favours the variant, with $2{,}000$-draw
bootstraps resampling respondents (households on \dataset{LPMC}), all of a cluster's events
moving together and the same resampled clusters used across variants. Intervals condition on
the trained models and the observed split. Five prespecified contrasts form a Holm-corrected
confirmatory family; the rest is exploratory, and an interval containing zero is never read as
equivalence.
