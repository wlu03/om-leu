\section{Introduction}

A growing body of work replaces or augments the hand-specified attributes of a behavioural
model with text produced by a language model: a short description of what each available
option would mean for the individual facing the decision. The text is embedded by a frozen
sentence encoder, read by a small learned network, and combined with a conventional model.
Reported gains are real in the sense that the combined system scores better than the
conventional model alone. What is usually left open is \emph{why}.

Three explanations are compatible with the same measurement, and they have different
consequences for what one should build next.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Ensembling.} Combining two models that make partly independent errors improves
      a proper score, whatever the second model reads. If this is the explanation, the text is
      incidental and a second numeric model would do as well.
\item \textbf{Information.} The text carries something the attributes do not -- a reading of
      the situation, a consequence the tabulated columns do not encode. This is the claim the
      literature usually makes.
\item \textbf{Architecture.} The gain comes from how the text is read: axis-wise scoring,
      attention over sentences, person-specific valuation. This is what most method sections
      describe at length.
\end{enumerate}

We take an existing three-stage pipeline of this kind and measure all three quantities
separately. The pipeline is not our contribution and we do not argue for it; it is a
representative instance, and holding it fixed is what makes the comparisons clean. Every
variant in this paper sees the same generated sentences, the same encoder, the same
conventional model and the same ensembling budget. Only the named component changes.

Our findings are largely corrective.

\textbf{A matched channel with no text attracts more weight than the text.} The fitted
mixture weight on the language channel is $0.21$--$0.40$ across datasets, but a
parameter-matched numeric channel with no language input at all receives $0.37$--$0.52$
(\Cref{tab:pi}). The mixture weight is a measure of wanting a second opinion, not of
linguistic content, and should not be reported as evidence for the latter.

\textbf{The reader architecture is not the source of the gain.} An ordinary two-layer network
on the same embeddings matches the designed reader on all three travel datasets and beats it
on two (\Cref{tab:structural}). Inside the designed reader, the component we had described as
modelling individual taste -- person-specific weights over named attribute heads -- can be
replaced by a constant with no measurable effect on the deployed system, and we give the
measurement that explains why (\Cref{sec:why-weights}).

\textbf{A control the field does not use overturns one positive result.} Shuffled sentences,
identity-only text and random embeddings are all trained models producing non-uniform
predictions; none of them isolates the fact that mixing \emph{anything} into a sharp model
bounds its loss. Replacing the language channel by the uniform distribution at the same
fitted weight is strictly stronger. Two of our four datasets pass the usual three controls
and fail this one (\Cref{tab:floor}).

\textbf{The effect is protocol-dependent.} On the single dataset whose ordering field is
genuine calendar time, a time-respecting split reverses the ranking: the shuffled control
beats the real text and a text-free channel beats both (\Cref{tab:protocol}). We also show
that two of our three datasets cannot support a time-respecting protocol at all, because
their apparent timestamps are a questionnaire task index and a date manufactured by a
preparation script.

Alongside these we report four proposals that did not work, each with the diagnostic that
explains the failure rather than a bare number, and an audit of the generation inputs which
found statistics derived from the respondent's own choices inside the prompt. All numbers in
this paper are recomputed from per-event predictions saved for every run; the script that
writes every table is released with the code.
