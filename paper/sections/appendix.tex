\section{Why a component can matter alone and not in the pool}
\label{app:weights}
\label{sec:why-weights}

The largest knock-out in \Cref{tab:knockouts} disappears when the channel is pooled, and the
explanation is measurable. The personalisation is genuine: the learned weights vary across
people (standard deviation $0.23$ on one head, range $0.92$, $65\%$ sharing a top-weighted
head), and a variant that learns \emph{one} weight vector shared by everybody recovers none of
the loss ($0.5626$ against $0.5628$ for fixed uniform weights and $0.5262$ for the personalised
version). The loss is a tail rather than a shift: the worst tenth of events carries $+0.97$ on
\dataset{Optima} and $+0.53$ on \dataset{LPMC} while the rest carries $+0.05$ and $-0.02$, and
those tail events are ones the attribute model already ranks correctly ($72\%$ and $67\%$).
\Cref{eq:floor} bounds what such a failure can cost in the pool, and the first-order sensitivity
is the standalone change multiplied by $\rho = \pi q_y/p_y$, which collapses exactly where the
ablated channel collapses. Measured attenuation: $25.9\times$ on \dataset{Optima},
$21.9\times$ on \dataset{LPMC}. The component raises average accuracy without changing which
events the text channel wins -- its win rate is $36.2\%$ before the ablation and $36.1\%$ after
-- and that is what a probability pool discards.

\section{Limitations in full}
\label{app:limits}

\paragraph{Limitations.} The travel datasets are public case studies with a few hundred to a
thousand respondents, and intervals condition on the observed split. The \dataset{Amazon} run
uses a smaller generator on a hundred-respondent fixture, so it establishes the bound effect in
a second domain but nothing quantitative across domains. Two arms are incomplete. A prompt
designed to separate supported outcomes from unverified judgements is written and validated but
not run at scale, for want of a generation budget; what we can report is that a mechanical scan
of the existing text finds $2.4$--$26.3\%$ of sentences asserting claims the supplied evidence
does not establish (\Cref{sec:audit}). And an extension representing outcomes as distributions
rather than points is implemented and tested on synthetic fixtures with no empirical arm, since
no dataset here records repeated journeys, scenario probabilities or elicited beliefs.

\paragraph{The design that would settle it.} Withhold a designated subset of attributes from
the generator, so that the surviving contribution is identified by an exclusion restriction
rather than by a linear projection, and elicit from people the consequences they actually
considered, so that the word \emph{perceived} is tied to a measurement. Neither is run here.
Together they are what would convert a conditional predictive claim into a claim about
perception.

\section{Audit of the generation inputs}
\label{sec:audit}

Before running anything we audited what the generator is shown and what it writes.

\paragraph{Label-derived statistics reach the prompt.} The person profile supplied to the
generator is rendered from a per-respondent row containing a count of that respondent's events
and the mean of a per-event novelty indicator. Both are aggregates over \emph{all} of that
respondent's events, including their validation and test events, and the second is a function of
the chosen alternative. The rendered phrases are coarse, so the channel is weak, but it is
label-derived and future-derived; our grounded allowlist rejects both fields programmatically.

\paragraph{Unsupported claims in the generated sentences.} A mechanical scan of $120$ events per
dataset counts sentences matching claim types the supplied evidence cannot establish -- calling a
price negligible, asserting parking availability, stating a preference rather than an outcome --
and numerals that match no supplied quantity under a declared unit conversion.

The claim scan is a lower bound, since it only catches the patterns it knows; the numeral check
is an upper bound, since a correctly derived total counts as unsupported. Whether a sentence is a
fair reading of the evidence is not mechanically checkable and remains unverified: we release a
label-blind annotation export with fields for support, factual correctness, preference
contamination and ambiguity, and note that no annotator has scored it.

\paragraph{Other audit findings.} Attribute standardisation in the released pipeline was fitted on
a training split that is not the training partition of a respondent-disjoint fold; we
re-standardise inside each fitting partition, which is exact because standardisation is affine.
The generation cache is keyed by a composite that folds hashes of the person text and the rendered
alternative into the version string, which we verified empirically: no two events of a respondent
share an embedding for the same alternative. Admissible history uses strictly prior counts. No
dataset carries an availability mask, so all alternatives are treated as available, which is an
assumption inherited from the source case studies rather than a property of the data.

\section{Calibration details}
\label{app:calibration}

The two scalars are fitted by L-BFGS with a strong Wolfe line search in double precision,
initialised at $\gamma = -6$ so that the model starts as the conventional model alone. Every fit
is checked at both boundaries ($\pi = 0$ and $\pi = 1$) for finiteness and against a
$21 \times 21$ grid over $(\pi, \log a)$; the grid never found a better optimum than L-BFGS in any
reported run. Fitting on a single validation split rather than out of fold moves the fitted weight
by up to $0.3$ and costs $0.010$ nats on \dataset{Optima}. On \dataset{Amazon} the conventional
channel becomes extremely sharp -- fitted inverse temperature $153$ -- and sixteen out-of-fold
fits overflowed; those are recorded as failures rather than dropped.

\section{Tests}
\label{app:tests}

Fifty acceptance tests accompany the code. They cover: rejection of label-derived fields in a
generation input; respondent and household disjointness, and disjointness of development folds;
forward-only temporal blocks and rejection of a task index or a manufactured date as calendar
time; a single application of the temperature; finite loss at both mixture boundaries; masked
probabilities summing to one with zero mass on unavailable alternatives; locality of each axis
score to its own sentence slot and the identity $\mathrm{d}u = s\,w_k\,\mathrm{d}c_k$; equivariance
to permuting alternatives; claim preservation of paraphrases (quantities, negation polarity,
evidence status); the mixture gradient identity against autograd and the complementarity
derivative against a finite difference; positive semi-definiteness of the covariance in the
distributional extension and its exact reduction at zero risk aversion; frozen-parameter checks in
transfer; and the monotonicity decomposition of \Cref{sec:gate}.

Four defects were found and fixed during the study, each with a regression test: a covariate
re-standardisation that divided by a near-zero deviation and silently made one dataset's
conventional channel uniform; a reporting path that averaged archived runs into current tables;
transfer variants that all ran with a single dataset as target and were therefore self-transfers;
and a crash in the report writer. We mention them because each was caught by a control or a test
rather than by inspection of the numbers.
