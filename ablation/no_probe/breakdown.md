**Problem:**  
Train the members without the InfoNCE probe, $\lambda = 0$ in
$\mathcal{L} = -\log p(y_i \mid E_i, z_i) + \lambda\big[-\log \operatorname{softmax}_j(c^{\top}\bar H_{ij})_{y_i}\big]$. What is the probe for, and what does its removal test?

**Explanation:**  
The probe is a linear classifier on the mean projected sentence $\bar H_{ij} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \mathrm{LN}(W e_{ijk})$ trained by the same conditional-logit loss as the member: within each choice set, the chosen alternative's mean sentence must score above the unchosen ones. Its gradient reaches only $W$ (and the LayerNorm), so it is an auxiliary objective that shapes the projected space, an InfoNCE contrast in which the positives are the chosen alternatives and the negatives the other alternatives in the same set. The main loss reaches $W$ too, but through the attention, the heads and the person weights, all of which are randomly initialised; early in training that gradient is small and noisy (the same dead-zone argument as for the multiplicative gate in `docs/math/04_stage3_semantic_mixture.md`, in milder form). The probe gives $W$ a direct, well-conditioned signal from the first step, so the projection becomes choice-discriminative before the rest of the member has learned to use it.

Removing it tests whether that warm start matters once early stopping has run its course. If the sentence-only $\Delta$ is near zero, the main loss suffices and the probe can be dropped from the description of the method. If it is positive, the probe is part of why the member trains at all on a few thousand events, and the reported gain of the sentence channel depends on it. The probe adds one vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$ and is discarded at inference, so the ablation changes the training procedure and nothing in the model that is evaluated; this is the one folder in `ablation/` whose change is to the objective rather than to the architecture.

Notation and the full sentence model are in `ablation/full_model/breakdown.md`; the paired ΔNLL and its bootstrap interval are defined in `docs/math/05_paired_evaluation.md`.
